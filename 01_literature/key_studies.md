# HIPPCON: key studies on hippocampal and cortical functional connectivity

Search date: 2026-09-24. Scope: human hippocampus (whole, long-axis segments, or subfields) used as a seed or ROI, with cortical or whole-brain connectivity measured by meta-analytic co-activation, resting-state fMRI, or task-based fMRI.

**How this list was verified.** Each PMID below was re-fetched with NCBI E-utilities (efetch/esummary) on 2026-09-24. Title, first author, year and DOI were checked against the record, and each DOI was searched back to its PMID (`raw/key_studies_verification.csv`). Nothing here comes from memory alone.
- The **Method, N, seed and cortical targets** columns come from the PubMed abstract.
- Where the open-access full text was available through the PMC/E-utilities API, details were added from it and marked **[FT]**.
- "NR" means not reported in the abstract, with no open full text available to check. These cells are left blank on purpose rather than guessed.
- Where the database or software of a meta-analysis could not be confirmed from the abstract or an accessible full text, this is stated explicitly.

Columns: Study (PMID / DOI) | Method | N / data | Seed definition | Main cortical targets reported | Relevance

## Gaps in the literature (for positioning a new study)

1. **No pooled synthesis of the connectivity literature itself.** Across 3,511 screened records we found no meta-analysis that pools **hippocampal-seed resting-state or PPI connectivity findings** in healthy adults. The existing "meta-analytic connectivity" is MACM, meaning co-activation derived from activation databases. Meanwhile, this search found **119 eligible reports (plus 2 through other methods)** that report hippocampal-seed whole-brain or cortex-wide connectivity in healthy adults: 65 rsFC, 43 task-FC, 8 mixed and 3 meta-analytic. Another **152 reports are pending full-text checks** (`prisma_counts.json`).
2. **One database per study, and no cross-database replication.** Chase 2015 used BrainMap; Grady 2020 and Vogel 2020 used Neurosynth. From abstracts and available full texts, no hippocampal MACM or CBP study replicated its findings across BrainMap and Neurosynth/NeuroQuery (or NiMARE-based pipelines). For Robinson 2015/2016 and Plachti 2019 the database could not be verified at all.
3. **Coarse subregion contrasts in meta-analytic work.** Meta-analytic studies stop at anterior/posterior or at 2-5 data-driven clusters. Chase 2015 restricted the seed to the subiculum and notes the resolution limits of BrainMap. No meta-analysis contrasts subfields (CA1, CA2/3, DG, subiculum), or head, body and tail, against cortical targets with formal subtraction or conjunction. Subfield-by-long-axis connectivity (Vos de Wael 2018; Dalton 2019; Ezama 2021; Chang 2021) comes from single datasets, mostly HCP, and in Dalton 2019 is limited to MTL ROIs.
4. **Task vs rest disagree, and nobody has reconciled them.** Fritch 2025 (HCP rest: anterior→default, posterior→attention) is the reverse of Fritch 2021 (task gPPI). Robinson 2021 (PMID 34790106) found that task-based and resting-state hippocampal clustering differ. Raud 2023 reports stable profiles across states. A single analysis comparing MACM, rsFC and task-FC maps with the same seeds is missing.
5. **Gradients vs parcels is unresolved at the meta-analytic level.** Gradient evidence (Przeździk 2019; Vos de Wael 2018; Bayrak 2022; Nordin 2025) comes from rsFC in single cohorts. Meta-analytic CBP enforces discrete clusters.
6. **Laterality and age are rarely modelled.** Only Robinson 2016 tests hemisphere × long axis meta-analytically. Age effects (Blum 2014; Damoiseaux 2016; Nordin 2025; Fenerci 2024) have never been examined as moderators across studies.
7. **Seeds are defined inconsistently.** Seeds come from atlases, the uncal apex, tractography, ICA, or task activation. A new study should standardise seeds, for example with HippUnfold/HippoMaps subfields and long-axis bins, and report sensitivity to the choice.
